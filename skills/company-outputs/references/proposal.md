# Proposal authoring

Read the complete brief and preserve source section/page citations. For a tender, map each requirement to the proposed response, evidence, dependencies and (when relevant) pricing linkage. Agent-assigned IDs must be labelled as internal, not client identifiers.

Explain the client's problem, the recommended solution and why it fits, delivery work, sequence, responsibilities, acceptance measures, assumptions and exclusions. A template sets presentation, not the answer. Make tradeoffs and dependencies concrete; do not claim delivery capacity, availability, references or credentials without evidence.

A missing required credential remains an explicit compliance gap with an owner and the decision needed. Drafting confirmation can accept disclosure of that gap; it cannot establish compliance. Separate proposed milestones from confirmed supplier commitments. Scope/solution changes require a revised plan and confirmation before writing the changed deliverable.

Use the selected company's visual language and medium. Inspect representative reference layouts and actual long-output sections, tables and final page/surface. Check requirement coverage and narrative coherence independently of template fidelity. Report absent attachments, unverified claims and rendering limitations. A visually polished response with missing mandatory evidence is still a review draft.
